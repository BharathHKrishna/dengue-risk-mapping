# Model card: climate and environment based dengue risk model

**Status: research prototype.** Not a validated operational forecast. Not for
clinical, individual-level or resource allocation decisions without local
validation and review by public health experts.

## Model details
- Author: Bharath Harikrishna (personal portfolio project, public data only).
- Type: LightGBM gradient boosted trees (regression on `log1p` monthly
  incidence per 100,000), plus LightGBM quantile models (5th and 95th
  percentiles) with split conformal calibration (CQR).
- Variants:
  - `lgbm_climate`: climate lags, seasonality, static land and population
    covariates. No past case data. This is the deployed model.
  - `lgbm_ar`: same plus past log incidence (lags 1, 2, 3, 12 months).
    Evaluated for comparison only, because past cases are not available in
    real time for India and are delayed in Brazil.
- Seeds: 0, 1, 2. The deployed point prediction is the 3-seed mean.
- Code version: see the git hash logged with every MLflow run.

## Intended use
- Relative, month-ahead risk mapping for dengue from climate and environment:
  "which places and months look more favourable for transmission".
- Teaching and discussion material on spatial transfer and validation.

## Out of scope
- Forecasting exact case counts.
- Any decision at district level in India: no district case data exist to
  validate the model there.
- Areas outside the climate range seen in Brazil (for example the Himalaya,
  arid western Rajasthan) without caution.

## Inputs
Lagged (1 to 3 months) ERA5-Land temperature (mean, min, max), relative
humidity, ERA5-Land and CHIRPS rainfall, MODIS daytime land surface
temperature; month of year (sine, cosine); GHSL population density and built
surface, JRC surface water occurrence, MODIS NDVI, VIIRS night lights, SRTM
elevation, ESA WorldCover class shares. Full list and licenses:
[DATA_SOURCES.md](DATA_SOURCES.md).

A prediction for month `t` uses only data up to month `t - 1`.

## Training data
InfoDengue notified dengue cases for all 5,570 Brazilian municipalities,
aggregated from epidemiological weeks to calendar months (each week counted in
the month of its Wednesday, counts scaled to a 4.345-week month). Population
from InfoDengue (IBGE estimates).

## Evaluation
See [README](../README.md#results) and `results/tables.md` (generated by
`scripts/make_tables.py` from CSVs written by the pipeline). Evaluations:
- Temporal hold-out: train 2015 to 2021, calibrate 2022, test 2023 to 2025.
- Spatial blocked CV: hold out each of Brazil's 5 macro-regions, train on the
  other 4 (training years only), test on the held-out region in 2023 to 2025.
- Transfer: Brazil-trained model predicts Indian districts, aggregated to
  states and years, compared by rank with OpenDengue (NCVBDC) reported cases.
- Uncertainty: empirical coverage of 90% conformal intervals.
- Metrics: MAE, RMSE, R2, skill versus seasonal climatology, Spearman, and for
  the high-incidence alert: precision, recall, F1, AUC-PR. 95% CIs from a
  municipality cluster bootstrap (1,000 resamples).

### Headline performance (deployed climate-only model; full tables in results/tables.md)
- Temporal hold-out 2023 to 2025: MAE 1.821, skill vs local climatology 0.012
  (95% CI 0.006 to 0.017). With climatology as an input, climate adds -0.008.
- Held-out Brazilian regions: skill vs pooled climatology from -0.046 (North)
  to 0.357 (Center-West); worse than local history in 4 of 5 regions.
- India state ranks 2015 to 2024: mean Spearman 0.146, below population
  density alone (0.258). National seasonal timing: Spearman 0.855 (2024).
- 90% conformal intervals: coverage 0.831 (temporal), 0.741 to 0.978 (held-out
  regions).

## Known limitations and data gaps
- 24.7% of India district-months (442 of 641 districts) have inputs outside the
  Brazil training range (mostly maximum temperature and elevation). These are
  flagged in the API (`inputs_outside_training_range`) and on the map.
- Elevation acts as a proxy for Brazil's high-burden central plateau, not as
  biology, and drives the largest India errors (Uttarakhand).
- Labels are notified cases, which depend on testing, reporting and
  healthcare access. Under-reporting differs strongly between places and
  between Brazil and India.
- InfoDengue population is nearly constant over time per municipality (two
  distinct values per municipality in the pull), so incidence trends partly
  reflect population growth not captured.
- India population uses a single GHSL epoch (2020) for all years.
- India districts are the 2011 Census set; later splits are not represented.
- West Bengal has no OpenDengue record for 2018 and 2019 and an unusually low
  2024 value (441 cases), which is likely incomplete reporting.
- Night lights use 2022 (the first year of the VIIRS V22 annual collection).
- Static covariates are single snapshots and do not track urban growth.
- Dengue serotype shifts, immunity and vector control are not modelled. These
  drive large multi-year swings (for example Brazil's 2024 epidemic) that a
  climate model cannot fully explain.
- Conformal intervals assume calibration and test months are exchangeable.
  Year-to-year shifts in dengue break this, so coverage on later years can
  fall below the nominal level (reported in the results).

## Ethical considerations
- Maps can stigmatize places. The map shows modeled relative risk and says so
  on the map itself.
- India boundaries follow the Survey of India official external boundary
  (DataMeet layers).
- No individual-level or private data are used.
